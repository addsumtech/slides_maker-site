# Bright Classroom · Corporate Training Template (worked example: *Structured Reporting*)

## Template File
`template.pptx` (16:9, 16 pages, English, natively editable; per-page renders in `render/slide01.png … render/slide16.png`, build script in `build_corporate_training.py`)

## Summary
A **bright, classroom-style** template for **adult learning / corporate training / workshop decks**, built out to full depth around a course you could actually teach — *Structured Reporting* — as **real example content**: course goals and audience, why "I can't get my point across" is a genuine problem, an overview of how the three methods fit together, SCQA / Pyramid / MECE taught one at a time with a "before / after" rewrite page each, two live drills, a five-trap recap, a one-page cheat sheet, an action list and a sources page.

Where it fits: **corporate training, onboarding, workshops, internal brown-bags, certification courses, train-the-trainer, university survey courses** — any teaching material that has to **explain first, get people writing in the room, and send them home with a checklist they can act on**, in a dozen or so pages. It works both as a **projected deck** and as a **hand-out**: every page carries a spoken script in the speaker notes, and every page still reads on its own without a presenter.

**The point: real example content + a reusable visual system.** Every method taught here has a public, checkable source, cited line by line on P16 (Barbara Minto 1987 / 1996, a McKinsey alumni interview, Miller 1956, Cowan 2001), with the verification date printed on the page; anything that can't be sourced isn't claimed — accounts of where the term "elevator pitch" comes from differ, so the deck borrows the format and says in print that it **makes no claim about the origin**. The emails, reports and category lists are **fictional teaching scenarios written to demonstrate structure**, flagged in the top right of every page they appear on and declared again on P16; none of it is dressed up as real company data. What's actually reusable is the **teaching layout language** — swap in your own course content and you get material of the same quality.

The one thing this template sets out to prove: **teaching rhythm can be built into the visuals.** Learners shouldn't need to be told "we're doing an exercise now" — the page says it:

- **Three functional colours, one meaning each** — teaching blue = instruction / mustard yellow = practice / brick red = caution. The same three run through the footer badge, the bottom session strip and in-page emphasis, and **there is no fourth semantic colour** (the cover and the sources page sit in a neutral register and go grey; grey carries no meaning).
- **A 16-cell session strip along the bottom (the quiet signature, on every page)** — one cell per page, coloured by that page's mode; the current cell grows taller, goes solid, and carries the page number. Verified page by page: the current cell advances exactly one step per page and always agrees with the footer badge. On any page, that bottom edge is a map of the whole session: the two yellow cells are where you do the work. **The two practice cells and the caution cell stay fully saturated on interior pages; the instruction cells and the neutral cells drop to a pale tint** — landmarks have to be readable at any distance, otherwise the ruler decays into a decorative dotted line nobody decodes. The cover blows the strip up, draws cell 1 in the "current page" style and adds a three-colour legend, so learners learn to read it at first glance.
- **Practice pages and teaching pages are told apart in one look** — teaching pages are white with hairlines and a right-hand rail; **practice pages become one big sticky note**: mustard bled to the footer, dog-eared corner, none of the teaching pages' eyebrow-and-rule furniture, instructions moved into a white card on the right instead of a rail, a larger title (28pt against 21pt). There is exactly one line on the yellow ground — the 5.0in black rule under the P12 timer, which is the visual anchor for "hard stop", not layout furniture. You can't mistake one page for the other from the back row.
- **A "before / after" two-column component reused across the deck** — left = grey draft paper + ✕, centre = a blue circular arrow, right = white paper + teaching-blue outline + ✓, bottom = a "Difference" annotation bar. It appears three times (P06 / P08 / P10) with **the outer frame pixel-identical** — the two cards, the circular arrow and the annotation bar sit at exactly the same coordinates and sizes — so the three pages can be compared side by side and what learners see is the content difference, not a layout difference. Inside the "after" column the arrangement follows the content: P06 and P10 are four evenly spaced entries, P08 is one conclusion plus three numbered reasons.

**The signature move (a controlled aesthetic risk): the "answer-first band"** — a full-bleed colour band **replaces** the page title: the topic shrinks to a small eyebrow inside the band and **the one-sentence answer becomes the page's own headline**. On all four pages that use it, what sits in the title slot *is* that page's answer rather than its topic: **the form of the deck is the method it teaches.** Worth saying what the device is **not**: it isn't a banner laid on top of a title, which would only eat the eyebrow's space and leave "topic as headline" intact. Here the band **eats the title itself** — so what it buys is not vertical space (measured on the renders, the four banded pages start their content column at 1.31–1.42in and the band-less pages at 1.01–1.51in: the same interval, no displacement) but the **semantics of the title slot**: a page gets only one biggest sentence, and this device forces you to hand it to the answer. P05, where it first appears, carries a self-referential note — "← this line is 'answer first'" — that teaches the device itself.

Recoloured brick red, the same device becomes the header of **P13, the caution page** — so each of the three registers has a canvas-level signature: **instruction = white + blue band / practice = full yellow + dog-ear / caution = white + red band**. In a thumbnail grid you can tell three kinds of page apart, not just "the yellow ones" and "the rest".

Every page is a worked example of one "teaching move → layout":
- **Oversized step numerals** (01 / 02 / 03 at 60pt — the number is the anchor of the page)
- **A chunking compressor** (12 loose pieces → the compressor → 3 chunks, turning "working memory can't hold this" into a spatial fact)
- **A pyramid tree** (conclusion block → three same-kind reasons → six fact blocks, with connector weight separating trunk from tips)
- **Three segmented rulers** (MECE: one row can only carry one cut — mutual exclusivity and exhaustiveness drawn at the same time, which a table can't do)
- **Hero timers** (`15` at 72pt / `00:30` at 108pt on the practice pages — P12 makes time the protagonist; P11 deliberately inverts it, shrinking the timer so the three steps lead)
- **A five-row "✕ wrong → ✓ right → rule badge" table** (every trap points back at the rule it breaks)
- **Three cheat-sheet cards** (the one page in the deck where side-by-side cards own the whole canvas — cards elsewhere are supporting rails or tick-boxes; the middle one is deliberately taller and solid, because the Pyramid owns the spine)
- **The sticky-note motif** (a small sticky = a prompt, a full-page sticky = practice)

## Structure (page by page)
01. **Cover** — course name + three-part promise + length / format / output; a yellow sticky on the right says "bring a status report"; the session strip is enlarged into a rhythm map of the whole session with a "Teach / Practice / Caution" legend
02. **What this course is** — oversized 01/02/03 for the three goals (answer first / group like with like / order it right) + a right rail with "who it's for" (three icon rows) and "what it's not"; one closing line at the bottom, the single test for the session
03. **Why people can't get their point across** — 12 loose pieces → a blue "compressor" band → 3 chunks; a brick-red note at the bottom explains that Miller 1956 and Cowan 2001 are **findings about memory capacity, not a layout rule about bullets per slide**
04. **The three, in one picture** — one diagram for how the methods divide the work: SCQA owns the opening, the Pyramid owns the spine, MECE owns how each group is cut; a short note on the order you use them
05. **Method 1 · SCQA** — **first appearance of the signature move** (blue answer-first band + self-referential note); four S/C/Q/A rows, each with a giant initial and a self-check question, with C's check highlighted
06. **SCQA · the complication is a date** — first appearance of the before/after component (eyebrow "before / after ① " with the scenario); the title is a claim, not a topic label, and the word counts are measured from the body text, so the numbers on the page always match what's printed
07. **Method 2 · The Pyramid** — answer-first band + pyramid tree (conclusion → three same-kind reasons → six fact blocks) + a right rail with the three rules; one "how to read it" line underneath
08. **Pyramid · three categories → one** — same component (eyebrow "before / after ② · asking for two more people"), drilling rule ② (one group, one kind)
09. **Method 3 · MECE** — answer-first band + three segmented "cut" bars (by lifecycle / by owner / by deal size). The fourth cell of the first bar, "External", is deliberately drawn as an **outline** cell: it isn't another notch on that ruler, it's the **catch-all** kept so the cut is exhaustive, and it should look like a different kind of thing. The deal-size bands read "1M and above / 100K to under 1M / Under 100K" — endpoints that don't overlap, because the page that teaches mutual exclusivity has to be mutually exclusive itself
10. **MECE · two cuts → one cut + catch-all** — same component (eyebrow "before / after ③"), drilling exclusivity and exhaustiveness
11. **Live practice ① (first appearance of the practice register)** — full mustard + dog-ear; hero `15` minutes + three steps (on your own / in pairs / whole room) + a white instruction card "peer review: three questions" + a black output bar
12. **Live practice ② (pitch it in 30 seconds)** — same register, inverted composition: the hero `00:30` leads and the instructions retreat into the right-hand card; the title states the task outright (a practice page can't leave people asking "what are we doing?"), and a footnote records that the origin of "elevator pitch" is not being claimed
13. **Common traps (caution register)** — full-bleed brick-red header (the same device as the blue answer-first band, in the caution colour); five rows of "✕ wrong line → ✓ fix → which rule it breaks", with the five badges in the right column covering all three rules plus SCQA and MECE — a complete trap ↔ rule map; one line to close
14. **The cheat sheet** — three cards (SCQA / Pyramid / MECE), each with what it owns, its key terms, when to use it and a self-check question; the middle card is taller and solid
15. **Wrap-up and action list** — the big claim "Structure isn't decoration. It's there to save them the work." + four tick-boxes for the next 14 days + a yellow sticky
16. **Sources** — five numbered, checkable sources + a right-hand card with four scope notes (fiction disclosure / elevator-pitch origin / Miller's own closing remark that he only *suspected* the 7 was a coincidence / why "answer first" is the top line rather than a fourth rule) + the verification date

## Fonts & Colors
**Type (role-based pairing, deliberately only two families)**
- Latin / figures: **Avenir Next** (geometric humanist sans; gentle enough for teaching without going soft; lining figures, so `01` / `15` / `00:30` and the step numerals never bounce or break across lines)
- CJK fallback tagged on every run: **Hiragino Sans GB** (safe on macOS, clear weights; falls back to Microsoft YaHei / Noto Sans CJK SC cross-platform). **PingFang is deliberately avoided** — LibreOffice renders it as a handwriting face
- **There is no third typeface.** Eyebrows, footers, scope notes and badges are all Avenir Next, separated by **weight and tracking** — eyebrows get 1.6–2.0pt of tracking, and footers and scope notes on white drop to the third ink (the ones sitting on a colour band switch to that band's pale tint instead — see the colour section)
- Size tokens: `9 eyebrow/scope · 11 in-card detail · 12 example body · 13 body · 16 subhead · 21 page title / answer-first band · 28 big statement / practice title` plus the display sizes `38 cover (42 in the Chinese twin) · 60 step numeral · 72 / 108 practice timers`
- `12` is a **deliberate role size**: the sample copy inside the before/after cards — a passage the whole room reads together, so it has to hold up from the back — while `11` is reserved for reference detail read up close. The two coexisting on one page isn't size sprawl, it's role separation
- Every run carries an `<a:ea>` CJK font tag, so mixed English/CJK never falls back or tofus if the deck is later localised

**Colour (a semantic contract — one colour, one meaning, all the way through)**
- Canvas: **pure white `#FFFFFF`**; primary ink `#1A1A1A` (17.4:1) · secondary `#55555A` (7.4:1) · third `#6E6E75` (5.1:1) · hairline `#E3E3E0`
- **Teaching blue `#1A56C4` = instruction** (6.6:1 on white; white type on it is also 6.6:1). Used for: the answer-first band, the pyramid and framework diagrams, the "after" column, the footer instruction badge, the instruction cells of the session strip. Deep blue `#0F3F9E`, mid-deep `#2A66CE`, bright `#2E77F0` and mid blue `#3A73D4` (4.58:1) are fill variants of the same family — they appear only in colour blocks and on the oversized step numerals, and **are never body-text colours**
- **Mustard `#F5C542` = practice / your turn** (primary ink on it is 10.7:1). Used for: full-bleed practice pages, the sticky-note motif, the practice cells of the session strip. Deep mustard `#E0AE28` only ever draws a dog-ear; the two dividers inside the white practice cards are `#C08A00` (the short heavy rule under the card title) and `#E0E0DC` (the hairline above the footnote). **Yellow-family type on white is always `#7A5200`** (6.9:1); the mustard itself sets type only on the black bars, where it reads 10.7:1 (the P11 step numerals and "OUTPUT", the P12 "SCORE")
- **Brick red `#B23A26` = caution** (6.0:1 on white). Used for: the common-traps page, scope callouts, the caution cell of the session strip; the pale type sitting on the brick-red header is `#F8E6E2` (4.9:1). **It appears only where learners need to be careful, never as decoration**
- Draft paper `#F4F4F2` + draft ink `#6B6B70` (4.8:1) = a muted pair reserved for the "before" column, so the old draft looks like a page that's been handled
- **Green is deliberately unused**: "after" is teaching blue rather than the reflexive green — blue already means "the thing being taught" in this deck, and the rewrite *is* the demonstration. Adding green would take the colour system from three meanings to four and break the contract
- Each of the three registers has a pale tint used on the footer badge: blue `#E9F0FE` / yellow `#FDF4DA` / red `#FBEAE6`, so the badge looks like one component across all 16 pages. The blue tint doubles as the fill for cards and callout bars (the sticky-note motif takes no tint — full saturation is the point). Two more pale blues are pure fill (`#C8D6F0` for the 12 loose pieces on P03, `#C6D6F4` for the through-baseline under the P09 cut bars), and two pale blues set type on teaching blue: `#C5D7F8` (4.56:1, the lowest contrast point in the deck) and `#D3E1FB` (5.0:1, the eyebrow and self-referential note inside the band). On the session strip, non-current instruction cells drop to `#A9C1EE` and neutral cells to `#DEDEDA`, while practice yellow and caution red stay saturated. Body text on the full-yellow practice pages is `#4A4238` (6.1:1 on `#F5C542`)
- Every piece of body text clears 4.5:1 against the surface it sits on — all **439 text runs** measured, lowest 4.56:1 — with the values annotated line by line at the top of the build script; meaning is never carried by colour alone (a practice page is coded four ways at once — full yellow, dog-ear, larger type, footer badge)

**Layout**: a 12-column grid (0.58in symmetric outer margins, 0.14in gutters). **The bottom edge is deliberately uneven** — against a content floor of 4.86in, P04 stops at 4.09in (the diagram stops when it's finished), P15 at 4.23in (the close) and P14 at 4.51in (the cheat sheet), leaving real void below. `design_intent` records the intent on five pages: `envelope="upper"` on P04 / P07 / P15 (on P07 only the left column rides high — the three-rule rail on the right still runs to the floor, so the marker records the intent, not the result) and `envelope="full-bleed"` on the practice pages P11 / P12; P14 is the one page that stops high without a marker. The closing line rotates position too: a full-width filled bar (P06 / P08 / P10 — "Difference" is a fixed part of the comparison component) / a 0.05in vertical rule plus one line (P02 / P05 / P07 / P09 / P13 at 13pt, P03's scope note at 9pt in brick red) / a right-column note (P04) / an eyebrow above the title (P14) / no closing line at all (P11 / P12 / P15 / P16), so not every page ends on the same full-width takeaway. Four title treatments rotate, never twice in a row: T1 the answer-first band (blue on P05/P07/P09; the same device recoloured brick red on P13), T2 the left rule + title (P03/P06/P08/P10/P14), T3 eyebrow + hairline (P02/P04/P16), T4 full yellow with no rule (practice pages P11/P12), with the cover and wrap-up (P01/P15) on their own display treatment. The most-used treatment still accounts for only 31%, so no single one turns into a template tic; the only adjacent repeat is P11/P12, which is intentional — the two drills belong together. Every auto-growing text block (annotation bars, in-card copy, the sources column) has its position derived from a `measure_text` reading and worked back from the bottom of the canvas, never hand-placed; the build ends with `lint_layout(strict=True)`, which raises on any critical geometry fault and refuses to save.

**Diagrams and graphics**: all native shapes and text, editable element by element in PowerPoint. **No AI-generated imagery** — the visual weight is carried entirely by the colour system and native geometry; mood images would dilute the whole selling point, that the teaching rhythm is built into the layout. Icons are a single **Phosphor duotone** family, recoloured to whatever they sit on and rasterised — 7 in total: five in teaching blue (three on the P02 rail card, two on the white P14 cards) and two in white (on the P13 brick-red header and on the solid blue P14 card).

**English edition note**: this deck is the English twin of the Chinese build — same layout, same palette, same components, same 16 pages, same per-page structure. Only the copy changed. Three page-local fit adjustments were needed where English simply runs longer than Chinese, and all three are annotated in the build script: the cover title drops from 42pt to 38pt so it keeps its air from the sticky note; the SCQA word column on P05 widens from 1.30in to 1.70in into empty space so "Complication" stays on one line; and the cheat-sheet card terms on P14 drop from 13pt to 11pt so both columns stay single-line. The `sub_w` argument on the T2 titles (the width actually reserved for the top-right scope note) was retuned per page, which is what that parameter exists for. Everything else in the script is untouched code.

**Portability**: a `.pptx` stores only font names. Opened on a machine without Avenir Next, the deck falls back to the system sans (metrics shift slightly; the grid and layout hold). This template uses the macOS system fonts Avenir Next and Hiragino Sans GB; on Windows, substitute Segoe UI / Century Gothic (+ Microsoft YaHei for any CJK), or install Avenir Next / Noto Sans CJK SC for identical results. **PingFang is not used anywhere in the deck** — LibreOffice renders it as a handwriting face. To change anything, edit `build_corporate_training.py` and re-run rather than hand-editing the binary, so the deck stays reproducible.
